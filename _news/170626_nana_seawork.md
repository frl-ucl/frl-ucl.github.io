We are pleased to share that **Nana Kutin** attended **Seawork 2026** and was awarded **third place** for his research project on maritime decarbonisation.

Nana's project, titled *"Decarbonising the UK Fleet: A Life Cycle Assessment Software Tool for Alternative Marine Fuels"*, was developed under the supervision of **Dr Yuanchang Liu, Dr Peng Wu, and Prof. Richard Bucknall**.

:small_blue_diamond: **Research Project**
The project introduces a web-based Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) platform designed to quantify maritime fleet emissions and evaluate alternative-fuel decarbonisation scenarios against emerging International Maritime Organization (IMO) and European Union (EU) regulatory requirements.

The platform integrates three main components: an activity-based emissions inventory derived from Automatic Identification System (AIS) data, a structured catalogue of alternative fuel pathways, and a scenario engine that projects fleet emissions towards 2050. It considers a range of marine fuels, including methanol, ammonia, hydrogen, biofuels, and LNG, supporting the assessment of different pathways towards lower-emission shipping.

:small_blue_diamond: **Industry Impact**
The project addresses the growing need for practical tools to support shipowners and maritime stakeholders in navigating increasingly stringent emissions regulations. By combining operational vessel data, fuel life cycle assessments, and future regulatory targets, the platform enables users to compare alternative-fuel strategies and evaluate their potential environmental and operational implications.

With applications extending to shipowners, charterers, fuel suppliers, port authorities, and classification societies, the tool aims to support informed decision-making and accelerate the transition towards a more sustainable maritime industry.

We are proud of Nana's achievement at Seawork 2026 and congratulate him on receiving third place. This recognition highlights the importance of research-driven innovation in maritime decarbonisation and the growing contribution of FRL research to the future of sustainable shipping.
